Project Brief

The Yucatan Peninsula is a three-state territory, host of a living network of karst aquifers known as cenotes, which act as the sole water source in the region. The interconnected network leads to the ocean while providing water to the cities of Valladolid, Merida, smaller adjacent towns, and many others.

Because the water table in the Yucatan Peninsula is a key element and major water source for people and other living beings in the territory, the treatment of water and development of water infrastructure in this territory is empirical due to its environmental fragility. Cenotes are living ecosystems, made of porous karst, hosts of aquatic, terrestrial, and aviary species, and geological formations, which serve as natural chronologic tracking devices that are able to determine changes in climate.

The Cenote Research Institute’s mission is to carry out scientific research, develop human resources for cultural practices, distribute knowledge amongst local citizens, generate employment through training, and promote environmental practices.

The building and project master plan systematically integrate the community into a scientifically led field. The bio-sanctuary, community market, and plaza spaces are all interconnected by subtle design features and porous path networks that allow the user to explore and interact with the environment. The facility is a 47,000 sq. ft., three-story facility designed to balance cultural exhibits and laboratories dedicated to the preservation of water, geology, and biology.
